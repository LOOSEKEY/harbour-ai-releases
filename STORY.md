### It started as something I built for myself.

I never set out to build a company. I set out to solve my own problem.

I wanted to use AI for real work: drafting, summarising, sorting through documents, the genuinely
useful stuff. But I couldn't get past one thing. To use any of the big tools, you have to hand your
data over. Your files, your notes, your private work, all of it shipped off to someone else's server
in another country, to be stored and processed who-knows-how. For anything sensitive, that's a
non-starter. And nothing out there did it the way I thought it *should* be done.

So I built my own. Just for me. A private AI that ran entirely on my own machine at home, where
nothing ever left the building. It was never meant to be anything more than that: a tool for my own
use, kept to myself.

### Then I gave a copy to a friend.

A few days later, my phone rang. It was him.

*"Mate, this is really good. You need to build this properly and put it out there."*

And then he did the thing that changed everything: he sent me a list. Things to add, things to
improve, things he'd want if he were using it for real. He wasn't just being polite. He'd actually
used it, and he wanted *more* of it.

That was the moment a private tool quietly became a product. So I started building. One "wouldn't it
be handy if it could also…" at a time. The list grew. The tool grew. And somehow, here we are.

### Where I come from.

I grew up in Birmingham, and I started learning to code in the nineties. I've never stopped since:
from then right up to today, I've kept at it. The world has changed beyond recognition in that time,
with new languages, new tools and entirely new ways of building things.

It was never a straight line, mind. Along the way I've done plenty of jobs that had nothing to do with
computers. But all the way through, I've built things privately for people who needed them.
Software, mostly, made to order for whoever asked. That work has taken me to places most people
never will see. I've also built server rooms underground, for private clients and businesses alike.
When the machines that hold everything sit below ground, you learn very quickly what "keeping data
safe" really means, and you never forget it.

The *syntax* changes, the *tools* change, the trends come and go. But the way of thinking doesn't
expire: break the problem down, work out what's really going wrong, and be stubborn enough to keep
at it until it gives in. That part never left. Everything else, I picked up as I went.

### It isn't just me.

People ask what happens if the one person behind it is ill, away, or busy. The honest answer is that
there isn't just one person. My daughter, **Nevaeh Moore**, is 18, and she works with me side by
side. She knows HARBOUR AI inside out, as well as I do, and when I'm not around she's the one who
picks up the phone and answers the email.

And if neither of us were here tomorrow, your copy of HARBOUR would carry on. A bought key is checked
once, when you activate it, and never again. Nothing on your machine depends on us being around.

### What it is now.

What began as a private experiment at home is now a full local AI **platform**: five specialist
agents and 120+ business tools, all running entirely on your own computer. No cloud. No telemetry.
No subscription. Your data never leaves your machine.

And it goes further than "trust me." Every answer HARBOUR gives comes with a cryptographically
signed receipt, proof of which model produced it and when, backed by a tamper-evident audit log
you can verify yourself, independently, offline. Private AI you can actually stand behind.

It's used by a company here in the UK and in Germany, and by a growing number of people running it
on their own. It's grown so much that, honestly, there are tools in there I've never even used
myself. But every one of them is there because, at some point, someone wanted it.

HARBOUR came first, and everything else grew out of it. The same idea runs through all of them:

working with the internet unplugged. These are live and available to use today:

- **[EMMA](https://ownemma.com)**: a private AI that lives on your computer, remembers you, and

- **[Build Emma](https://buildemma.com)**: a local-first coding platform with a resident agent,

After everything, every late night, every bug, every feature, it still feels like *mine*. I think

If you've ever wanted AI for your work but couldn't bring yourself to send your data off to someone

I hope it's as useful to you as it's been to me.
